# Understanding Music Theory

Music theory gives names to patterns that musicians already hear, perform, and
create. This open textbook begins with the written language of music, connects
notation to the physics of sound, and then develops the ideas of scales,
intervals, harmony, form, and practical musicianship.

:::{tip} You do not need to be an expert music reader
New musicians can follow the book in order. Readers who already know basic
notation can begin with [Sound and Acoustics](chapters/ch-25-acoustics-for-music-theory.md)
or [Notes, Keys, and Scales](chapters/ch-28-octaves-and-the-major-minor-tonal-system.md).
:::

## What you will find

The lessons combine explanation, score examples, short listening files, worked
examples, and practice with solutions. The final part extends the fundamentals
through tuning systems, modes and ragas, and transposition.

This web edition adapts Catherine Schmidt-Jones’s *Understanding Basic Music
Theory* and its shorter predecessor, *Introduction to Music Theory*. The source
material is available under Creative Commons Attribution licenses; details and
conversion notes are recorded in [Sources and Attribution](SOURCES.md).
